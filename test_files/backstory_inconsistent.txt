Character Backstory: Eleanor Blackwood

Eleanor was born in 1895 in Manchester, the daughter of a wealthy textile merchant. She discovered her passion for clockmaking at age 30 when she moved to London.

She was known for her exceptional color vision, able to distinguish hundreds of shades that others couldn't perceive. This gift helped her select the perfect materials for her clock faces.

During World War I, Eleanor served as a nurse in France, where she met her husband Captain James Hartley. They married in 1918 and had two children together.

Eleanor loved sailing and spent her summers on her private yacht, exploring the English coastline. She learned to swim competitively and won several local championships.

After the war, she opened a clock shop in London's West End, commuting daily by automobile—she was one of the first women in England to obtain a driver's license.

She was famous for her telephone consultations, speaking with clients across Europe about their timepiece needs. Her workshop employed over fifty craftsmen at its peak.

Eleanor emigrated to America in 1945, settling in Boston where she lived until her death in 1980 at age 85.
